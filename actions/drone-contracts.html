Defense & War Profiteering

War, Drones, and the
Trump Family Wager

Weeks before the United States launched military operations against Iran, Eric and Donald Trump Jr. moved hundreds of millions of dollars into drone and defense technology companies directly positioned to profit from the conflict.

Compiled from verified reporting · February – March 2026 · Multiple sources

In February 2026 — the same month the United States initiated war with Iran — Eric Trump and Donald Trump Jr. made substantial investments in drone and defense technology companies. The transactions involve hundreds of millions of dollars channeled through a web of funds, holding companies, and affiliated organizations.

The timing is striking: the Trump Administration's military campaign against Iran has been defined by the pervasive use of drone technology on all sides. The same class of assets the family invested in weeks earlier now sits at the operational heart of an active war.

Beyond the ethical dimension, the investments expose structural conflicts of interest at the highest levels of the U.S. executive branch. The Trump family holds financial stakes in companies that are direct beneficiaries of the Pentagon contracts and defense procurement decisions that flow from the very administration their father leads.

One company, Powerus, is merging with a Trump-backed publicly traded vehicle and is explicitly targeting the market vacuum created by the administration's own ban on Chinese drones in the United States.

⚑
Editorial Note — Conflict of Interest

The investments described herein were made by the sons of the sitting U.S. President in companies that profit directly from military action ordered by that President. This is not an ordinary business story.

The influx of investment weeks before the Trump Administration launched a war heavily reliant on the same technology wreaks of blatant impropriety.

The Israeli drone company Xtend, in which Eric Trump holds a strategic stake, has been awarded military contracts from the Pentagon — the same Pentagon operating under his father's cabinet. The company is involved in a $1.5 billion merger with JFB Construction to go public. Dominari Securities, approximately 6% owned by each Trump son, organized a $44 million private placement tied to that deal.

Donald Trump Jr., who serves as a shareholder and advisor to Unusual Machines — a drone components supplier — has layered his exposure further: Unusual Machines both invests in and supplies Powerus, and also has involvement in the Xtend merger. The family's core investment vehicle, American Ventures, holds the primary stake in Powerus.
