FUNDRAISING BASED ON FALSE ELECTION CLAIMS

Between November 2020 and January 2021, Donald J. Trump and his campaign apparatus raised hundreds of millions of dollars from supporters by soliciting funds under the pretext of “defending election integrity” — in other words, to fund legal challenges, recounts, or other efforts to overturn the 2020 election outcome.

According to the findings of the United States House Select Committee on the January 6 Attack, the “official Election Defense Fund” repeatedly referenced in fundraising emails and appeals never formally existed. Investigators found no evidence that the “election defense fund” ever functioned as promised — i.e. no account or record showing money spent on serious election litigation or recounts.. According to the J6 Committee, nearly $100 million was raised in the first week after the election, and in total roughly $250 million was solicited under the “fraud / defend the election” narrative.

Instead of deploying the money toward genuine legal challenges, recounts, or legitimate election-related litigation, the bulk of the funds were diverted to political committees and entities under Trump’s control — particularly Save America PAC. From there the money was used for other political operations, payments to consultants, and contributions to pro-Trump organizations.

From Save America, only a small portion of funds were spent on legal fees. The bulk was used for:

  • Political operations and consulting firms — payments to consulting and strategy vendors tied to former Trump campaign officials.
  • Rally and event organization — including January 6-related activities managed by Event Strategies Inc. and similar vendors.
  • Transfers to pro-Trump organizations and think tanks — e.g., America First Policy Institute ($1M), Conservative Partnership Institute ($1M).
  • Payments to Trump-owned businesses — Mar-a-Lago, Trump Hotels (~$327,000+), covering event space, lodging, catering, and venue fees.
  • Cash reserves for future use — tens of millions held in Save America PAC.

Some specific transactions include:

  • Event Strategies Inc. — organized rallies and events, including January 6: $10 million.
  • Trump-owned businesses — Mar‑a‑Lago and Trump Hotels: ~$327,000+, covering event space, lodging, catering, and venue fees.
  • America First Policy Institute — think tank connected to many former Trump officials: $1 million.
  • Conservative Partnership Institute — organization tied to ex-Chief of Staff Mark Meadows: $1 million.
  • Legal fees / law firms — tens of millions (exact unknown) covering post-election lawsuits, congressional investigations, and related legal matters filed against Trump.
  • National Public Affairs, LLC & other consulting firms — consulting and “political operations” payments tied to campaign allies: $140,000+.

Legal Implications & Applicability of Federal Laws

18 U.S.C. § 1343 — Wire Fraud
This is the federal wire fraud statute, which criminalizes schemes to defraud people of money or property by means of false or fraudulent pretenses, using interstate wires or electronic communications. Because the initial claims prompting donors to contribute were knowingly false — and used to elicit donations — there is a credible argument that the fundraising constituted a fraudulent scheme. Under federal law, that could trigger prosecution under wire-fraud statutes (e.g. 18 U.S.C. § 1343). Many legal analysts consider this a strong candidate for criminal charges.

18 U.S.C. § 371 — Conspiracy to Defraud the United States
The statute addressing conspiracy to defraud the United States (or to commit offense against United States laws). If campaign staff or advisers coordinated fundraising based on known false claims with intent to defraud donors or misuse funds, that could qualify as a conspiracy under § 371. Some legal commentaries analyzing the J6 findings point to this possibility.
