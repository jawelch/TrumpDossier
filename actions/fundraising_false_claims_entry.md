# FUNDRAISING BASED ON FALSE ELECTION CLAIMS

Between November 2020 and January 2021, Donald J. Trump and his campaign apparatus raised **hundreds of millions of dollars** from supporters by soliciting funds under the pretext of “defending election integrity.” According to the United States House Select Committee on the January 6 Attack, the “official Election Defense Fund” referenced in appeals **never existed**. Instead, most of the money was routed to political committees under Trump’s control, particularly Save America PAC, rather than being used for election litigation or recounts.

Investigators found that nearly **$250 million** was raised through these false fraud-based appeals. Funds were spent on political operations, consultants, contributions to pro-Trump organizations, and operational expenses — not on legal challenges to the 2020 election.

A scholarly analysis titled **“The Perils of Fundraising Using the Disinformation of the Big Lie”** characterizes the fundraising effort as “a fraud within a fraud,” built on disinformation and false claims of a fund that did not exist.

The January 6 Committee’s final report documented that the false fraud narrative and the associated fundraising campaign were part of a coordinated post-election strategy, raising potential legal exposure.

## How the laws may apply:

### **18 U.S.C. § 1343 — Wire Fraud**
Soliciting money using knowingly false statements through electronic communications (email, online platforms, digital fundraising) may constitute wire fraud.

### **18 U.S.C. § 371 — Conspiracy to Defraud the United States**
If multiple individuals coordinated fundraising efforts while knowing the underlying election-fraud claims were false, the scheme may constitute conspiracy to defraud the United States.
