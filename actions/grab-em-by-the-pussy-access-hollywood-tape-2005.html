“Grab ’Em by the Pussy” Recording

In October 2016, just weeks before the presidential election, a 2005 recording surfaced of Donald Trump speaking to television host Billy Bush while preparing to film an episode of Access Hollywood.

In the recording, Trump described attempting to have sex with a married woman and stated that, because he was a celebrity, he could “do anything,” including grabbing women “by the pussy” without their consent.

The recording was published by The Washington Post and quickly became a central issue in the 2016 presidential campaign. The remarks were widely condemned as endorsing sexual assault and normalizing non-consensual sexual contact.

Trump later released a statement calling the comments “locker room talk,” but the recording prompted bipartisan criticism and led multiple elected officials to withdraw or reconsider their endorsements.

Legal and Ethical Concerns

Admission of Sexual Assault

The statement describing the ability to grab women without consent was interpreted by many legal analysts as describing conduct that would constitute sexual assault under numerous state laws.

Character and Fitness Concerns

The recording raised broader concerns about judgment, respect for consent, and suitability for public office. Critics argued that the language reflected a pattern of conduct toward women that was inconsistent with professional and ethical standards expected of elected officials.

While the recording itself did not result in criminal charges, it became a defining moment of the 2016 election and contributed to ongoing public debate about accountability, misconduct, and standards for public leadership.
