Amplifying Racist Theory About Haitian Immigrants in Springfield, Ohio

September 2024

In September 2024, during the presidential election, Donald Trump and his allies amplified the false conspiracy that Haitian immigrants were stealing and eating pets in Springfield, Ohio. During a presidential debate with Vice President Kamala Harris, Trump said, “In Springfield, they're eating the dogs, the people that came in, they're eating the cats. They're eating, they're eating the pets of the people that live there. And this is what's happening in our country, and it's a shame."

These rumors were thoroughly debunked. City, county and state officials said that there was no credible reports or evidence to support the conspiracy. The city's mayor and the governor of Ohio, both Republicans, denounced them. The original poster of the theory on Facebook deleted their post and expressed regret that the story had spread. The city of Springfield has since filed a lawsuit against a neo-Nazi group for driving the threats against Haitian immigrants.

The biggest proponent of the theory was then-vice presidential nominee and Ohio senator JD Vance. Later, when pressed on the claims by reporters, Vance said, "If I have to create stories so that the American media actually pays attention to the suffering of the American people, then that's what I'm going to do."

The claims had dramatic effects on the Springfield community. Ohio governor Mike DeWine said authorities were forced to investigate at least 33 bomb threats in the area — many accompanied by anti-Haitian messages — as a result of the hoax. Haitians and the immigrant community as a whole in Springfield and other American cities reported harassment, racist attacks, and vandalism because of the controversy. Residents said they could not leave their homes for fear of being attacked. Others said they would be moving for their safety. White supremacist groups like the Proud Boys and neo-Nazis marched in Springfield and Klu Klux Klan flyers were spotted in town. Government officials in the city and journalists covering the story received death threats and were swatted. The mayor of Springfield said the additional measures implemented to respond to public safety concerns cost the small city hundreds of thousands of dollars.

The false conspiracy about Haitians in Springfield was clearly a racist falsehood developed by white supremacists to attack immigrant communities. Trump and Vance's endorsement of the theory — with the latter admitting it was a lie — knowingly gave credibility to the groups pushing bigotry and purposefully fueled this hatred.
