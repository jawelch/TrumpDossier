John Kelly Warns Trump Meets the Definition of a Fascist

October 22, 2024

In October 2024, former White House chief of staff John Kelly issued some of the most direct and forceful warnings from a senior Trump insider. In interviews with The New York Times ahead of the 2024 election, Kelly said his firsthand experience convinced him that Donald Trump meets the definition of a fascist. Kelly was Trump’s longest serving chief of staff in his first term, giving him unmatched access to the president’s conduct and governing style.

When asked directly whether Trump was a fascist, Kelly read aloud a standard definition he had found online: “It’s a far-right authoritarian, ultranationalist political ideology and movement characterized by a dictatorial leader, centralized autocracy, militarism, forcible suppression of opposition, belief in a natural social hierarchy.”

Kelly then stated that definition accurately describes Trump. “So certainly, in my experience, those are the kinds of things that he thinks would work better in terms of running America.”

He expanded further: “The former president is in the far-right area, he’s certainly an authoritarian, admires people who are dictators — he has said that. So he certainly falls into the general definition of fascist, for sure.” He added that Trump “certainly prefers the dictator approach to government.”

Kelly also said Trump never accepted the limits of presidential power. “He never accepted the fact that he wasn’t the most powerful man in the world — and by power, I mean an ability to do anything he wanted, anytime he wanted.”

Kelly described early clashes in 2017 when he informed Trump that senior officials swore an oath to the Constitution rather than to him personally. Trump resisted the idea, pressing Kelly repeatedly and showing no appreciation that government leaders are bound to the rule of law above personal loyalty.

These warnings formed the basis for the subsequent October 2024 letter signed by thirteen former Trump officials, who urged voters to take Kelly’s assessment seriously.
