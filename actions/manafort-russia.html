Republican-Led Senate Panel Finds Trump Campaign Participated in and Benefited from Russian Interference

August 2020

In August 2020, a Republican-controlled Senate Intelligence Committee released a bipartisan report concluding that Russia pursued an aggressive interference campaign on behalf of the Trump campaign during the 2016 election — and that the campaign not only benefited from that interference, but actively participated in it.

The report's most significant finding concerned Paul Manafort, Trump's campaign chairman. Manafort had regular contact with Konstantin Kilimnik, a Russian intelligence officer. He shared internal campaign polling data with Kilimnik during the height of the election, and the committee found evidence suggesting Manafort was connected to Russia's operation to hack and leak Democratic Party emails. This was not a peripheral figure — Manafort was the public face of the campaign, its senior strategist, and Trump's chosen representative at the Republican National Convention.

Manafort's entanglement with Russian interests predated the campaign. He had spent years working for Viktor Yanukovych, the pro-Kremlin Ukrainian president who was driven from office and fled to Russia following the 2014 Euromaidan uprising. That relationship formed the basis of several criminal convictions: in 2018, Manafort pleaded guilty to conspiracy against the United States, obstruction of justice, and witness tampering in connection with the Mueller investigation. He was separately convicted of tax fraud, bank fraud, and failure to register as a foreign agent for his undisclosed lobbying work on behalf of Yanukovych. In 2020, Trump pardoned him.

The significance of this report cannot be overstated. It was not produced by Democrats, nor by a special counsel appointed under political pressure. It was the conclusion of Trump's own party, on the Senate's most sensitive intelligence committee, after years of investigation. Their finding was that the Trump campaign accepted and encouraged interference from a hostile foreign government in an American election.

Trump's pardon of Manafort completed the picture. A man who admitted to conspiring against the United States, who shared campaign intelligence with a Russian officer, who obstructed the investigation into those very acts — was pardoned by the candidate who benefited from his work. The claim that Trump was unaware of what his campaign chairman was doing is not credible. The pardon makes clear he had no interest in accountability even after the fact.
