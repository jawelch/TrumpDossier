Nazi, White Supremacist-Aligned Rhetoric About Immigrants

2024

Donald Trump's attacks on the immigrant community in America have been a staple of his political career. His most heinous arguments have aligned with and mimicked Nazi and white supremacist ideology. Trump's rhetoric on immigrants became more aggressive during his time out of power and was a key component to his comeback in the 2024 election.

In 2023, Trump began repeatedly saying that immigrants were "poisoning the blood of our country," a phrase first made prominent by Adolf Hitler in his 1925 manifesto Mein Kampf. Hitler wrote the following:

  • "All great cultures of the past perished only because the originally creative race died out from blood poisoning."
  • "Whenever Aryans have mingled their blood with that of an inferior race, the result has been the downfall of the people who were the standard-bearers of a higher culture."
  • "He poisons the blood of others but preserves his own blood unadulterated." Hitler wrote of Jewish males allowing Jewish females to marry white Christians.

Trump, despite using the phrase repeatedly in speeches and on social media, said its usage had no correlation to Hitler's ideas. Yet the context Hitler penned the phrase strongly resembles the 'outsider' fearmongering of Trump and the "Great Replacement Theory" pushed by many of his allies.

During the same year, Trump also began to describe his political enemies as "vermin," which was often used by both Hitler and Mussolini to disparage Jews and political opponents. In a Truth Social post, Trump said "we will root out the Communists, Marxists, Fascists, and Radical Left Thugs that live like vermin within the confines of our Country." This choice of vocabulary is a textbook attempt to dehumanize people and radicalize supporters to "sanitization" or "extermination" the opposition. The tactic was similarly used during the 1994 Rwandan genocide, when the perpetrators used a term meaning "cockroaches" to dehumanize the Tutsi population.
