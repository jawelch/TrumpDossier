Trump Disparages Pope, Posts AI Image of Himself as Jesus Christ

April 2026

During the Iran War, Pope Leo XIV called on world leaders to step back from escalation, warning that those promoting war have "hands full of blood" and that God rejects such violence. President Trump responded by calling the Pope "soft on crime," labeling him part of the "radical left," and insisting the head of the Catholic Church should stay out of politics. Trump then posted an AI-generated image depicting himself as Jesus Christ healing a sick man. He later deleted the post and claimed the figure in the image was a doctor.

The sequence is worth sitting with. The leader of 1.4 billion Catholics called for peace during an active war. Trump's response was not a theological disagreement or a policy rebuttal — it was the same dismissive political language he applies to domestic opponents: soft, radical left, stay in your lane. The Pope's position — that war is a moral catastrophe and that God rejects violence — is not a controversial doctrinal stance. It is among the most foundational teachings of the Catholic faith Trump publicly professes.

The AI image compounded the contradiction. Depicting oneself as Jesus Christ — the central figure of Christian worship — is, by any serious reading of Christian theology, either blasphemy or idolatry. Religious scholars noted the image crossed a line that most believers across denominations would recognize immediately. That Trump deleted it and offered the implausible explanation that the figure was a doctor suggests even he understood the reaction it would provoke.

Trump has long positioned himself as a champion of Christian values and religious freedom. The record here tells a different story: a pope speaks from the core of Christian teaching and is called a radical leftist; the savior of that faith is borrowed as a personal image. What Trump demonstrates is not religiousness but its performance — the symbols and the language deployed selectively, and discarded just as quickly when they become inconvenient.
