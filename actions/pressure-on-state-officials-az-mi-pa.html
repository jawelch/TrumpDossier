Pressure on State Officials in Arizona, Michigan, and Pennsylvania

Following the 2020 presidential election, Donald Trump directly contacted, publicly pressured, or coordinated efforts to influence state officials in Arizona, Michigan, and Pennsylvania to overturn or delay certification of lawful election results.

These efforts targeted governors, secretaries of state, state legislators, and county election officials and sought to induce them to use their official powers unlawfully to change election outcomes Trump had lost.

Michigan

After Michigan certified its election results, Trump and his allies focused pressure on Republican state legislators and county election officials.

In November 2020, Trump invited Michigan Senate Majority Leader Mike Shirkey and House Speaker Lee Chatfield to the White House after the state had already certified Joe Biden’s victory. Trump publicly suggested the legislators could intervene to overturn the results despite having no legal authority to do so.

At the county level, Trump allies pressured Republican members of the Wayne County Board of Canvassers to refuse certification of Detroit’s election results. Two Republican canvassers initially voted to block certification citing unsubstantiated claims before reversing course following public backlash and legal warnings.

Michigan election officials later testified that these efforts had no factual basis and were intended to bypass lawful certification procedures.

Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, Trump targeted both state and local officials after losing the state by more than 80,000 votes.

Trump publicly and privately urged Pennsylvania lawmakers to “reexamine” ballots that had already been lawfully counted and certified. Courts repeatedly rejected his claims of widespread fraud.

Trump’s legal team encouraged legislators to delay or block certification by asserting the legislature could appoint its own slate of electors, an approach state officials rejected as unconstitutional.

Trump also called for special legislative sessions to overturn results even after certification deadlines had passed.

Arizona

In Arizona, Trump focused pressure on Governor Doug Ducey and state election officials during and after certification.

Trump publicly criticized Ducey and claimed the results were fraudulent without evidence. Ducey later confirmed he ignored calls from Trump while signing the certification documents.

Trump allies pressured Arizona legislators to decertify results or authorize partisan audits intended to cast doubt on the outcome. Arizona officials repeatedly affirmed the election was accurate.

State officials testified that Trump’s actions were aimed at creating delay and confusion rather than correcting legitimate errors.

How the Laws May Apply

18 U.S.C. § 1512(c)(2) — Attempting to influence or stop certification processes connected to Congress’s counting of electoral votes may qualify as obstruction of an official proceeding.

18 U.S.C. § 371 — Coordinated efforts to persuade officials to take unlawful actions may constitute a conspiracy to defraud the United States.

18 U.S.C. § 241 — Pressuring officials to reject lawful ballots may violate voters’ constitutional right to have their votes counted.
