Targets Democratic Congress Members With Investigation, Calls for Their Execution

November 2025

After six Democratic lawmakers released a video reminding military personnel of their duty to reject illegal orders, President Donald Trump called the message “SEDITUOUS BEHAVIOR punishable by DEATH.” He amplified another post stating, “HANG THEM GEORGE WASHINGTON WOULD !!”

The video was released in November 2025 by members of Congress who had previously served in the military or intelligence community, including Senator Mark Kelly of Arizona, a former Navy officer and astronaut, and Senator Elissa Slotkin of Michigan, a former CIA analyst who served multiple tours in Iraq.

The lawmakers’ message reflected longstanding constitutional principles. Members of the armed forces swear an oath to support and defend the Constitution of the United States, not to obey any individual officeholder. While the president serves as commander in chief under Article II, that authority is bounded by the Constitution and by laws enacted by Congress.

Article VI establishes that the Constitution and federal laws made pursuant to it are the supreme law of the land. Under the Uniform Code of Military Justice, service members are required to obey lawful orders. The requirement that orders be lawful is central to military justice. Orders that clearly violate the Constitution or federal law are not legally binding.

The rejection of blind obedience to manifestly unlawful orders has been firmly embedded in U.S. military doctrine since the Nuremberg tribunals after World War II. The principle that “just following orders” is not a defense to war crimes applies when an order is clearly illegal. Service members may be held individually responsible for carrying out unlawful acts.

Congress possesses explicit authority under Article I to raise, support, and regulate the armed forces. Members of Congress speaking about constitutional limits on executive power act within their legislative and oversight responsibilities. Civilian control of the military does not grant unchecked authority to the commander in chief; presidential power remains constrained by statute and the Constitution.

Trump’s Justice Department, led by U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia and former Fox News commentator Jeanine Pirro, attempted to pursue criminal charges against the lawmakers. Prosecutors were unable to secure an indictment.
