Trump’s Disparagement of John McCain, Disabled Veterans, and Troops Killed in Action

2015–2024

Donald Trump has a long, well-documented history of belittling U.S. service members who were captured, wounded, or killed. Senior officials who worked closely with him have described comments that they say reflect deep contempt for military sacrifice.

The most public example came in 2015, when Trump attacked Sen. John McCain’s military service during a campaign event:

“He’s not a war hero… he’s a war hero because he was captured. I like people that weren’t captured, okay?”

McCain, who spent more than five years as a prisoner of war in North Vietnam, was widely regarded as a symbol of endurance and sacrifice within the U.S. military community.

Behind the scenes, according to former White House chief of staff John Kelly and reporting by The New York Times, Trump repeatedly went further in private discussions. Kelly said Trump referred to service members who were injured, captured, or killed in action as “losers” and “suckers.”

Kelly described Trump’s hostility toward wounded veterans, including those who had lost limbs in combat:

“Certainly his not wanting to be seen with amputees — amputees that lost their limbs in defense of this country fighting for every American, him included, to protect them, but didn’t want to be seen with them. That’s an interesting perspective for the commander in chief to have.”

Kelly also said Trump would erupt into insults whenever McCain was mentioned:

“Whenever John McCain’s name came up, he’d go through this rant about him being a loser, and all those people were suckers, and why do you people think that people getting killed are heroes?”

Reflecting on Trump’s reported contempt for fallen troops, Kelly added:

“To me, I could never understand why he was that way — he may be the only American citizen that feels that way about those who gave their lives or served their country.”
