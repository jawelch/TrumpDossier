Trump Pardons Erase Over $1.5 Billion in Criminal Penalties

2025–2026

Clemency orders issued by Donald Trump eliminated or rendered unenforceable more than $1.5 billion in financial penalties owed by convicted criminals, including fines payable to the U.S. government and restitution owed to victims of financial crimes.

Analyses of pardons and commutations issued by Donald Trump estimate that more than $1.5 billion in financial penalties owed by convicted criminals were eliminated or rendered unenforceable. These penalties included fines payable to the U.S. government and restitution owed to victims of financial crimes such as fraud and tax evasion.

Many of the clemency orders specifically stated that outstanding financial penalties were to be forgiven. This differed from typical presidential commutations, which generally shorten or eliminate prison sentences but leave financial obligations such as fines or restitution in place.

Several high-profile beneficiaries of these pardons were individuals convicted of major financial crimes. Trevor Milton, the founder of the electric vehicle company Nikola, had been ordered to repay investors more than $600 million following fraud convictions. After receiving clemency, those repayment obligations were eliminated. Milton had previously donated nearly $2 million to political efforts supporting Trump prior to the pardon.

Another example is Lawrence Duran, who had been ordered to pay roughly $100 million after being convicted in a large Medicare fraud scheme. Clemency granted by Trump removed these financial penalties.

Hundreds of millions of the forgiven penalties consisted of criminal fines that would otherwise have gone to the U.S. Treasury. In other words, these funds were owed to the federal government and ultimately to taxpayers, in addition to restitution payments owed to victims of financial crimes.
