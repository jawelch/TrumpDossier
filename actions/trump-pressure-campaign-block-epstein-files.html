Pressure Campaign to Block Congressional Release of Epstein Information

2026

Reporting described a pressure campaign by Donald Trump and his allies aimed at discouraging congressional efforts to release additional records related to Jeffrey Epstein.

Reported by: The Atlantic (February 2026)

In 2026, members of Congress sought to release additional information and records connected to Jeffrey Epstein and related investigations. According to reporting by The Atlantic, Donald Trump and some of his allies attempted to discourage lawmakers from supporting the effort.

Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene said Trump personally urged her not to support efforts to release the information. According to the report, Greene said Trump argued that releasing the material could harm people he knew, telling her that “his friends would get hurt.”

The reporting also described Trump privately pressing aides and allies to stop the effort, repeatedly urging them to make the issue “go away.” Individuals familiar with the situation described Trump as frequently demanding that the matter be shut down before additional documents were made public.

Representative Thomas Massie was among the lawmakers pushing for the release of Epstein-related information. According to the report, staffers in Massie’s office were approached with offers of lucrative positions in a potential Trump administration or in the private sector, which Massie’s office viewed as attempts to weaken support for the effort within Congress.

The episode reflected broader tensions in Washington over whether additional records connected to Epstein should be released publicly and how much information from past investigations should be made available to Congress and the public.
