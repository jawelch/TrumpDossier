Trump Push to “Nationalize” Elections

February 2026

In February 2026, President Donald Trump publicly called for the Republican Party to “take over” and “nationalize” voting procedures while appearing on a podcast. Trump stated that the federal government should seize control of election administration in fifteen states. After White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt attempted to walk back the remarks, Trump reaffirmed and escalated his position.

A federal takeover of elections would be explicitly unconstitutional. The U.S. Constitution delegates election administration primarily to the states through Article I, Section 4, which governs congressional elections, and Article II, Section 1, which governs presidential elections. The Tenth Amendment further reinforces this authority by reserving powers not delegated to the federal government to the states.

Trump justified his proposal by claiming widespread fraud and corruption in election administration but provided no evidence to support these assertions. Since losing the 2020 election, Trump has repeatedly sought greater control over election processes, and a call for federal seizure of election administration represents a significant escalation of those efforts.

The constitutional structure placing elections under state control was deliberately designed to prevent abuse by the national government. James Madison warned that allowing federal officials to control elections would enable political power to select its own rulers. In Federalist No. 59, Madison described it as “extraordinary” and dangerous for representatives to regulate the elections that keep them in office, arguing that such a system would invite manipulation, entrenchment, and the erosion of republican government.
