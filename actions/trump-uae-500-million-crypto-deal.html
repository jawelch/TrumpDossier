Trump United Arab Emirates $500 Million Crypto Deal

January 2025

Days before Donald Trump returned to the presidency, a foreign national security official secretly acquired a massive ownership stake in a Trump linked cryptocurrency company, channeling hundreds of millions of dollars into Trump family entities. The transaction was concealed from the public, structured to avoid scrutiny, and followed by United States policy decisions that directly benefited the foreign investor’s government.

In January 2025, World Liberty Financial, a cryptocurrency venture tied to the Trump family, sold forty nine percent of the company for approximately five hundred million dollars. The buyer was an investment vehicle backed by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan, the United Arab Emirates’ national security adviser and head of its intelligence services. The deal closed just days before Trump’s inauguration.

The transaction was not publicly disclosed at the time. Eric Trump signed on behalf of Trump controlled entities. The ownership structure granted the UAE backed firm significant influence, including board representation, while avoiding the transparency typically expected for a company connected to a sitting president.

Roughly half of the purchase price was paid upfront, and a substantial portion of that money flowed directly into Trump family controlled companies rather than remaining within the operating business. Additional funds were directed to affiliates of Trump allies involved in the venture. This structure enabled immediate personal enrichment for individuals closely connected to the president, independent of the company’s future performance.

Sheikh Tahnoon is not a passive investor. He oversees intelligence, defense, and strategic investment operations for the United Arab Emirates. By acquiring a near controlling stake in a Trump linked firm, a foreign government official obtained financial leverage over the sitting president’s family business while maintaining formal deniability through intermediaries.

Months after the investment, the Trump administration approved the export of advanced United States artificial intelligence chips to the United Arab Emirates. These technologies had previously been restricted due to national security concerns. The approvals directly benefited UAE entities associated with Sheikh Tahnoon, prompting criticism that policy decisions appeared aligned with private financial arrangements rather than national interest.

Legal scholars and lawmakers have warned that the transaction may violate the Constitution’s emoluments clause, which prohibits presidents from receiving benefits from foreign states without congressional consent. Unlike traditional conflicts of interest, this arrangement involved a concealed transfer of wealth tied to a foreign intelligence chief immediately preceding the assumption of presidential power.

The episode illustrates a modern model of corruption in which foreign governments bypass formal diplomacy and instead invest directly in businesses tied to political leaders. By using opaque financial structures such as cryptocurrency ventures, influence can be obscured while policy outcomes quietly benefit the investor. This undermines democratic accountability and raises serious national security concerns.
