Withholds Funding for Gateway Project Until Penn Station and Dulles Airport Are Renamed “Trump” (2026)

In early 2026, multiple news outlets reported that President Donald Trump offered to unfreeze billions in federal infrastructure funding for a major New York–New Jersey tunnel project only if Penn Station in New York City and Washington’s Dulles International Airport were renamed in his honor. The proposal was conveyed during discussions with Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, who rejected the offer and stated that he lacked any authority to make such changes.

According to reporting, Trump told Schumer he would release approximately $16 billion in already-appropriated federal funds for the Gateway Hudson River tunnel and related infrastructure projects if the naming demands were met. Schumer described the proposal as unorthodox and lacking any legal basis.

The Gateway project is a critical transportation initiative intended to repair and expand aging rail infrastructure connecting New York and New Jersey. Construction had already begun when the administration froze funding. New York and New Jersey later sued the administration, arguing that the funding freeze itself was unlawful and threatened a long-planned and congressionally approved project.

Democratic lawmakers sharply criticized the move, characterizing it as vanity-driven and inappropriate. Several officials described the tactic as political hostage-taking or extortion. Senator Kirsten Gillibrand stated that naming rights are not negotiable conditions for receiving federal infrastructure funding, while other critics argued the demand amounted to using public funds for personal benefit rather than public good.

Legal and Constitutional Concerns

Legal scholars and watchdog groups noted that, beyond political controversy, the reported actions raise serious legal and constitutional issues.

First, the president does not have unilateral authority to withhold congressionally appropriated funds. Under U.S. law and constitutional principles, Congress retains exclusive control over federal spending.
